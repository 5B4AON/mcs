3.1  Sending Modes

The encoder converts typed text into perfectly timed Morse code. Audio plays through the sidetone speaker and key signals are sent through every enabled output (optocoupler, serial port).

ModeHow it worksBest for
Send on Enter Type your full message, then press Enter to send it all at once. Composing careful messages, contest exchanges
Live Each character is sent immediately as you type it. Casual QSOs, real-time ragchew
Practice The app generates a random sequence, plays it as Morse, and you type what you hear. After the round, per-character feedback shows your accuracy. CW copy training, building head-copy speed

Practice appears in the encoder mode dropdown when the Copy Practice card is enabled in Settings → Other. See § 3.5 for full details.

Transmit control: Click the transmit button (radio-wave icon) or press Enter in “Send on Enter” mode to begin sending. Click the button again to stop. In the full-screen conversation, pressing Escape stops active transmission; once stopped, press Escape again to close the view.

3.2  Encoder Speed

Adjust the Encoder WPM pill on the main screen to set your sending speed. The encoder uses standard PARIS timing (one PARIS word = 50 dit units).

The encoder WPM is independent of the keyer WPM and both decoder WPMs. This lets you compose text messages at one speed while manually keying at another.

3.3  Typing in the full-screen conversation

Click the ⛶ button in the main toolbar to open the Full-screen conversation. Use the Key / Type toggle beside Close to switch between mapped physical-key keying and text entry. Typed text appears inline with the conversation; the toggle never sends Morse. Your choice is remembered, so the next time you open the full-screen conversation it starts in the same mode (the default is Key). Switching to Type puts focus on the conversation so typing starts immediately (and raises the on-screen keyboard on phones); switching to Key closes it. Tab moves from the conversation to the play/transmit button, then through the toolbar buttons, then back to the conversation.

To show one typed letter at a time, large, with its dots and dashes, switch on Lens in the fullscreen kebab menu (see Lens mode in the Decoder chapter).

The full-screen conversation has one independent text buffer, separate from the main display. Switching Key/Type or closing and reopening fullscreen does not clear it. See § 2.5 for details on the clear-menu options.

On touch-capable devices, select Type message and tap the editor to open the on-screen keyboard. In Key mode, the configured mouse and touch keying surfaces remain available.

Example — Calling CQ

Set Encoder WPM to 20. Type CQ CQ CQ DE G3XYZ G3XYZ K and press Enter. The encoder sends perfect Morse at 20 WPM through your sidetone and any enabled key outputs to key your transmitter.

3.4  Prosign Support

You can type prosigns (procedural signals) directly in the encoder buffer using angle-bracket notation. For example, typing HELLO<SK> will send "HELLO" followed by the SK prosign (end of contact). Prosigns are recognized as single tokens and transmitted without inter-character spacing.

Supported prosign patterns:

  • <AR> — End of message
  • <AS> — Wait
  • <BT> — Break; pause
  • <KN> — Invitation to named station only
  • <KA> — Starting signal
  • <SK> — End of contact
  • <SOS> — International distress signal
  • <BK> — Break (invitation to transmit)
  • <HH> — Error; correction follows

Note: Some prosigns (AR, AS, BT, KN) share identical morse patterns with punctuation marks (+, &, =, (). You can type either the prosign pattern or the punctuation character — both will produce the same morse output. Enable Show Prosigns in Settings → Other to display prosign names instead of punctuation in the conversation logs.

When using WinKeyer output, clashing prosigns are automatically converted to their punctuation equivalents since the device only supports ASCII characters. Non-clashing prosigns (<SK>, <SOS>, <BK>, <HH>) are skipped in WinKeyer output.

3.5  Copy Practice

Copy Practice turns Morse Code Studio into a self-study CW trainer. Enable the feature in Settings → Other → Copy Practice, then select Practice from the encoder mode dropdown on the main screen or in the full-screen conversation toolbar.

How It Works

  1. The app generates a random sequence (characters, words, or callsigns) and feeds it through the encoder so you hear it as Morse audio.
  2. Depending on the feedback mode, the decoded text appears fully visible (Listen Only), blurred (Blurred and Type-Along), or blurred while you type what you hear in real time (Type-Along).
  3. In Type-Along mode, after the sequence finishes you press the validate button. The app compares your typed input to the reference using a Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) alignment and highlights each character as correct, incorrect, or missed, with an overall accuracy percentage. In Listen Only and Blurred modes there is no typed input or scoring — you practise copying by ear.

Content Modes

ModeDescription
Random Characters Groups of random characters drawn from a configurable pool of letters (A–Z), digits (0–9), and/or punctuation. Group size is adjustable from 1 to 5 characters.
Random Words Common English words, filterable by length (3, 4, and/or 5 letters). Words are drawn with a frequency bias toward more common words and avoid repeats within a session.
Random Callsigns Realistic amateur radio callsigns generated from an international prefix database with random suffixes — great for contest practice.

The Count setting (1–10) controls how many groups, words, or callsigns are generated per round.

Feedback Modes

ModeDescription
Type-Along (default) Decoded characters are blurred while you type what you hear in real time. When the sequence finishes, press the validate button (✓) to see per-character feedback with an accuracy percentage.
Blurred Audio plays and decoded characters appear blurred. Hold the reveal button to peek. Typing is unavailable in Listen and Blurred practice so the answer stays hidden. Choose Type-Along to enter what you hear. After the sequence finishes the text unblurs so you can check your copy. Press Next to start another round. No typed input or scoring is provided.
Listen Only Audio plays and decoded characters appear fully visible in the output area. Copy what you hear on paper or in your head. Typing is unavailable in Listen and Blurred practice so the answer stays hidden. Choose Type-Along to enter what you hear. After the sequence finishes, press Next to start another round. No typed input or scoring is provided.

Type-Along Rounds

The Rounds selector appears when Feedback is set to Type-Along. Choose 1 to keep the normal single-round exercise. Choose 2 to 5 to make a temporary practice session.

  1. Press Enter once to read the session briefing. No sequence plays yet.
  2. Press Enter again to start round 1. Type what you hear and press Enter after playback finishes to validate that round.
  3. Press Enter to start each following round.
  4. After validating the final round, press Enter to view each round's percentage and the average percentage.
  5. Press Enter again to return to the briefing for a new session. A further Enter starts its first round.

Round results are available only while the current practice session is open; they are not saved in settings or a history.

Pipeline

The Pipeline selector controls whether a practice round stays on this device or can use the configured output routes. The Direction selector is separate: it chooses whether practice text is displayed as RX or TX, and selects the RX or TX timing settings. It does not choose external output routes.

PipelinePractice DirectionWhat happens
Local RX Practice audio and vibration stay on this device. Text appears as RX and uses RX timing. Serial, MIDI, WinKeyer, and RTDB routes are not used.
Local TX Practice audio and vibration stay on this device. Text appears as TX and uses TX timing. Serial, MIDI, WinKeyer, and RTDB routes are not used.
Full RX Text appears as RX and uses RX timing. Enabled output routes may receive the round when they match the separate Encoder direction.
Full TX Text appears as TX and uses TX timing. Enabled output routes may receive the round when they match the separate Encoder direction.

Sending Practice to External Outputs

To route a practice round to external outputs, choose Full pipeline, then enable and configure the desired serial, MIDI, WinKeyer, or RTDB output. In Settings → Inputs → Keyboard Encoder, set Used for to RX or TX. Configure the external output to forward that same Encoder direction (or Both). For RTDB, also start its configured output channel.

The Copy Practice Direction setting does not need to match the external output. Use it to choose the displayed RX/TX label and timing; use the separate Keyboard Encoder Used for setting and each output's forwarding rule to control routing.

Additional Settings

  • Direction — controls whether practice characters appear as RX or TX in the conversation view, colour tagging, and timing. It does not control external output routing.
  • Name / Colour — optional display name and colour for practice characters in the fullscreen conversation view.

Fullscreen Practice UI

In the full-screen conversation, practice is available in Type mode and replaces the normal transmit button with playback controls. Switching the toggle to Key stops the practice session, clears its text and removes any blur. The practice brief, results and your Type-Along text appear at the top of the conversation, with your typing on the line directly under the challenge characters. On the main screen, practice accepts typing only: the keyboard keyer, mouse keyer and straight-key sprite are disabled until you leave Practice. “Clear all text areas” and “Clear this text area” also remove the practice brief and results.

  • Play (▶) — starts a new round.
  • Pause (▮▮) — pauses playback mid-sequence (finishes the current character).
  • Resume (▶) — continues from where you paused.
  • Next (▶) — when a round is finished, generates and starts a new sequence (all modes).
  • Validate (✓) — appears only in Type-Along mode. Compares your typed input to the reference and shows per-character feedback.

After validation in Type-Along mode, the reference text is displayed with each character colour-coded — green for correct, red for incorrect, and grey for missed — followed by an accuracy percentage.

Example — Beginner Copy Practice

Enable Copy Practice in Settings → Other. Set Content to Random Characters, Count to 5, Group size to 1, Feedback to Listen Only, and Encoder WPM to 15. Open the fullscreen encoder, select Practice mode from the kebab menu, and press Play. You will hear five individual characters while the decoded text appears on screen. Copy them on paper or in your head, then press Next to start a new round. Increase Count, Group size, or WPM as you improve.

Example — Contest Callsign Practice

Set Content to Random Callsigns, Count to 3, Feedback to Type-Along, and Encoder WPM to 25. Open the fullscreen encoder and press Play. Type each callsign as you hear it. When the sequence finishes, press the validate button to see which characters you got right.
