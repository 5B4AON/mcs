3.1  Sending Modes

The encoder converts typed text into perfectly timed Morse code. Audio plays through the sidetone speaker and key signals are sent through every enabled output (optocoupler, serial port).

While a character plays, its dots and dashes appear on screen in step with the tones, just like keyed symbols do (on the main screen after the output text, and in the full screen on the row below the text in Type mode). Characters received over Firebase RTDB show their symbols the same way. They stay hidden in the practice modes, and a blurred direction hides them until you reveal the text.

ModeHow it worksBest for
Send on Enter Type your full message, then press Enter to send it all at once. Composing careful messages, contest exchanges
Live Each character is sent immediately as you type it. Casual QSOs, real-time ragchew
Copy practice The app generates a random sequence, plays it as Morse, and you type what you hear. After the round, per-character feedback shows your accuracy. CW copy training, building head-copy speed
Send practice The app shows a random sequence as text and you key it with a key, paddle or other key input. After the round, per-character feedback shows your accuracy and your keying speed. Learning to send, building clean keying

The practice modes appear in the mode dropdown, under Practice, when the Copy Practice or Send Practice card is enabled in Settings → Other. See § 3.5 and § 3.6 for full details.

Transmit control: Click the transmit button (radio-wave icon) or press Enter in “Send on Enter” mode to begin sending. Click the button again to stop. While a message is sending in “Send on Enter” mode, pressing Enter with new text in the input box adds it to the end of the message; pressing Enter in an empty box pauses the send, and pressing it again resumes. In the full-screen conversation, pressing Escape stops active transmission; once stopped, press Escape again to close the view.

3.2  Encoder Speed

Adjust the Encoder WPM pill on the main screen to set your sending speed. The encoder uses standard PARIS timing (one PARIS word = 50 dit units).

The encoder WPM is independent of the keyer WPM and both decoder WPMs. This lets you compose text messages at one speed while manually keying at another.

Did you know?
Why the word PARIS?

Speed in words per minute needs a standard word, and PARIS is the one. Sent with the normal gaps, including the space after it, PARIS lasts exactly 50 dit lengths, so 20 WPM means one dit lasts 60 ms. It is also about as long as an average English word, which makes it a fair yardstick.

3.3  Typing in the full-screen conversation

Click the ⛶ button in the main toolbar to open the Full-screen conversation. Use the Key / Type toggle beside Close to switch between mapped physical-key keying and text entry. Typed text appears inline with the conversation; the toggle never sends Morse. Your choice is remembered, so the next time you open the full-screen conversation it starts in the same mode (the default is Key). Switching to Type puts focus on the conversation so typing starts immediately (and raises the on-screen keyboard on phones); switching to Key closes it. Tab moves from the conversation to the play/transmit button, then through the toolbar buttons, then back to the conversation.

In Send on Enter mode, text typed while a message is already sending waits as a new message, shown after the unsent text with a space between. Press Enter to add it to the end of the queue (the same as on the main screen); Enter on its own pauses and resumes the send. Live mode still sends each character as you type it.

To show one typed letter at a time, large, with its dots and dashes, switch on Lens in the fullscreen kebab menu (see Lens mode in the Decoder chapter).

The full-screen conversation has one independent text buffer, separate from the main display. Switching Key/Type or closing and reopening fullscreen does not clear it. See § 2.5 for details on the clear-menu options.

On touch-capable devices, select Type message and tap the editor to open the on-screen keyboard. In Key mode, the configured mouse and touch keying surfaces remain available.

Example — Calling CQ

Set Encoder WPM to 20. Type CQ CQ CQ DE G3XYZ G3XYZ K and press Enter. The encoder sends perfect Morse at 20 WPM through your sidetone and any enabled key outputs to key your transmitter.

3.4  Prosign Support

You can type prosigns (procedural signals) directly in the encoder buffer using angle-bracket notation. For example, typing HELLO<SK> will send "HELLO" followed by the SK prosign (end of contact). Prosigns are recognized as single tokens and transmitted without inter-character spacing.

Supported prosign patterns:

  • <AR> — End of message
  • <AS> — Wait
  • <BT> — Break; pause
  • <KN> — Invitation to named station only
  • <KA> — Starting signal
  • <SK> — End of contact
  • <SOS> — International distress signal
  • <BK> — Break (invitation to transmit)
  • <HH> — Error; correction follows
Did you know?
SOS is not three letters

SOS is one unbroken signal: three dits, three dahs, three dits, with no gaps between the letters. It was agreed internationally in 1906 simply because it is easy to recognise; “Save Our Souls” was a popular explanation added later. Before that, Marconi operators used CQD, which was sent as three separate letters.

Note: Some prosigns (AR, AS, BT, KN) share identical morse patterns with punctuation marks (+, &, =, (). You can type either the prosign pattern or the punctuation character — both will produce the same morse output. Enable Show Prosigns in Settings → Other to display prosign names instead of punctuation in the conversation logs.

When using WinKeyer output, clashing prosigns are automatically converted to their punctuation equivalents since the device only supports ASCII characters. Non-clashing prosigns (<SK>, <SOS>, <BK>, <HH>) are skipped in WinKeyer output.

3.5  Copy Practice

Copy Practice turns Morse Code Studio into a self-study CW trainer. Enable the feature in Settings → Other → Copy Practice, then select Copy practice from the Mode dropdown (under Practice) on the main screen. In the full-screen view the Key/Type toggle is locked to Type while Copy practice is the mode, because you type what you hear.

How It Works

  1. The app generates a random sequence (characters, words, or callsigns) and feeds it through the encoder so you hear it as Morse audio.
  2. Depending on the feedback mode, the decoded text appears fully visible (Listen Only), blurred (Blurred and Type-Along), or blurred while you type what you hear in real time (Type-Along).
  3. In Type-Along mode, after the sequence finishes you press the validate button. The app compares your typed input to the reference using a Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) alignment and highlights each character as correct, incorrect, or missed, with an overall accuracy percentage. In Listen Only and Blurred modes there is no typed input or scoring — you practise copying by ear.

Content Modes

ModeDescription
Random Characters Groups of random characters drawn from a configurable pool of letters (A–Z), digits (0–9), and/or punctuation. Group size is adjustable from 1 to 5 characters.
Random Words Common English words, filterable by length (3, 4, and/or 5 letters). Words are drawn with a frequency bias toward more common words and avoid repeats within a session.
Random Callsigns Realistic amateur radio callsigns generated from an international prefix database with random suffixes — great for contest practice.
Koch method Random characters from the first N lessons of the Koch method, with the newest lesson's characters coming up more often. See Koch Method below.
E-book A book, article or your own notes that you load from a file or a web address. It is played from where you stopped, with the text on screen. See § 3.7.
Did you know?
A callsign tells you where someone lives

The first letters of a callsign are a prefix assigned to a country, for example G for the United Kingdom, DL for Germany, JA for Japan and VK for Australia. Hearing a prefix tells an operator which part of the world is calling.

The Count setting (1–10) controls how many groups, words, or callsigns are generated per round.

Koch Method

Choose Koch method as the Content to learn the characters one lesson at a time. The Lesson selector lists the 40 lessons as “1 – K, M”, “2 – U” and so on up to “40 – X”. Lesson N plays random characters from lessons 1 to N, and the characters the lesson introduced come up about 30 % of the time, so the newest ones get the most practice. Move back and forth between lessons whenever you like; a new lesson applies from the next sequence.

  • Lesson help — the round ? button above the replay button (in Copy Practice and Send Practice, on the main screen and in full screen) plays the characters the lesson introduces (K and M in lesson 1, one new character in every later lesson) and shows each one with its dots and dashes in a see-through panel over the output area. The sound stays on this device. The first time you press Start for a lesson in a session, the help plays by itself; press Start again when you are ready and a 3 – 2 – 1 countdown runs before the round begins (press Start during the countdown to begin at once). After that the help plays only when you press the button (press it again to stop). It also works while a lesson is paused; resuming the lesson stops the help. In Copy Practice the button waits while a round is playing.
  • Choosing Koch method sets the group size to 5 and the Count to 10, about a minute of Morse at 12 WPM. You can change both.
  • The character pool options are not shown, because the lesson sets the pool. Koch practice is always Type-Along; the Feedback selector is hidden and your earlier choice returns when you pick another content type.
  • The hint under Rounds estimates how long a round takes at your speed and Farnsworth settings. Use 2 or 3 rounds to practise for a few minutes and see the average.
  • The punctuation characters of the later lessons are shown and scored as themselves; = is shown as =, not <BT>.
  • When a round, or the average of a session, scores above 90 %, the result recommends the next lesson with a Go to lesson button. The lesson never changes unless you press it.
  • Finish lesson 40 above 90 % and the course is complete: the output area darkens, the text turns amber and a firework show plays over a congratulation message, with the crack, rumble and crackle of each burst, until you clear the screen. In full screen on a phone the on-screen keyboard closes so the show has the whole screen.

Type-Along Rounds

The Rounds selector appears when Feedback is set to Type-Along (and always for Koch method). Choose 1 to keep the normal single-round exercise. Choose 2 to 5 to make a temporary practice session.

  1. Press Enter once to read the session briefing. No sequence plays yet.
  2. Press Enter again to start round 1. Type what you hear and press Enter after playback finishes to validate that round.
  3. Press Enter to start each following round.
  4. After validating the final round, press Enter to view each round's percentage and the average percentage.
  5. Press Enter again to return to the briefing for a new session. A further Enter starts its first round.

Round results are available only while the current practice session is open; they are not saved in settings or a history.

Did you know?
The Koch method

In the 1930s the German psychologist Ludwig Koch suggested learning Morse at full speed from the very first lesson, starting with just two characters. You add a new character only when you can copy the ones you know.

The Pipeline selector controls whether a practice round stays on this device or can use the configured output routes. The Direction selector is separate: it chooses whether practice text is displayed as RX or TX, and selects the RX or TX timing settings. It does not choose external output routes.

Sending Practice to External Outputs

To route a practice round to external outputs, choose Full pipeline, then enable and configure the desired serial, MIDI, WinKeyer, or RTDB output. In Settings → Inputs → Keyboard Encoder, set Used for to RX or TX. Configure the external output to forward that same Encoder direction (or Both). For RTDB, also start its configured output channel.

The Copy Practice Direction setting does not need to match the external output. Use it to choose the displayed RX/TX label and timing; use the separate Keyboard Encoder Used for setting and each output's forwarding rule to control routing.

Additional Settings

  • Direction — controls whether practice characters appear as RX or TX in the conversation view, colour tagging, and timing. It does not control external output routing.
  • Name / Colour — optional display name and colour for practice characters in the fullscreen conversation view.

Fullscreen Practice UI

In the full-screen conversation, practice is available in Type mode and replaces the normal transmit button with playback controls. Switching the toggle to Key stops the practice session, clears its text and removes any blur. The practice brief, results and your Type-Along text appear at the top of the conversation, with your typing on the line directly under the challenge characters. On the main screen, practice accepts typing only: the keyboard keyer, mouse keyer and straight-key sprite are disabled until you leave Practice. “Clear all text areas” and “Clear this text area” also remove the practice brief and results.

  • Play (▶) — starts a new round.
  • Pause (▮▮) — pauses playback mid-sequence (finishes the current character).
  • Resume (▶) — continues from where you paused.
  • Next (▶) — when a round is finished, generates and starts a new sequence (all modes).
  • Validate (✓) — appears only in Type-Along mode. Compares your typed input to the reference and shows per-character feedback.

After validation in Type-Along mode, the reference text is displayed with each character colour-coded — green for correct, red for incorrect, and grey for missed — followed by an accuracy percentage.

Example — Beginner Copy Practice

Enable Copy Practice in Settings → Other. Set Content to Random Characters, Count to 5, Group size to 1, Feedback to Listen Only, and Encoder WPM to 15. Open the fullscreen encoder, select Practice mode from the kebab menu, and press Play. You will hear five individual characters while the decoded text appears on screen. Copy them on paper or in your head, then press Next to start a new round. Increase Count, Group size, or WPM as you improve.

Example — Contest Callsign Practice

Set Content to Random Callsigns, Count to 3, Feedback to Type-Along, and Encoder WPM to 25. Open the fullscreen encoder and press Play. Type each callsign as you hear it. When the sequence finishes, press the validate button to see which characters you got right.

3.6  Send Practice

Send Practice is the mirror of Copy Practice. Instead of hearing Morse and typing it, the app shows a text and you key it with a straight key, a paddle or any other key input you choose. Enable it in Settings → Other → Send Practice, then pick Send practice in the Mode dropdown (under Practice) on the main screen. In the full-screen view it works in Key mode, with the same prompt box pinned at the top above the output. The Key/Type toggle is locked to Key while Send practice is the mode, and to Type while Copy practice is (your own choice comes back afterwards).

How a Round Works

  1. Press Start, or just key a character. A prompt card takes the place of the typing box and shows the text to key. The character to key next is shown bigger and blue.
  2. Key the text. Each character you key appears in the output area as it is decoded, and the dots and dashes in progress show as usual.
  3. The round ends by itself (see Check below), or you press the check button (✓). Characters appear correct, incorrect or missed, with the accuracy percentage and You keyed ≈ N WPM, the average speed of the characters you keyed.
  4. Press the button again, or simply key the next character, for the next text. With the Koch method, Go to lesson starts the first round of the next lesson at once.

The first picked key that produces a character owns the round, so two keys cannot mix their characters. An unreadable character counts as incorrect. Word gaps are never scored, so you do not have to time them.

Mistakes Need No Deleting

Everyone slips, and most people simply key the character again. Send Practice follows where you are in the text by lining up everything you have keyed with the whole prompt after each character: a wrong, repeated or skipped character is noticed, and the keying after it still counts. So you can leave a mistake in and carry on, or key a character again to put it right. When you have reached the end, or keyed as many characters as the prompt has, with mistakes left in, the round waits about three and a half seconds for corrections, and every new character restarts that wait, before it ends by itself. When it is clear you have finished (the last character you keyed is the prompt's last one, keyed right) it waits only one second.

Erasing a Mistake

Key the error prosign <HH> (eight dits) to erase the last character you keyed, as operators do on the air. It erases one character and never appears in the result.

Hear It First

The replay button beside the output area plays the character you are about to key (the bigger blue one) as Morse, so you learn how it sounds. It plays on this device only, never to serial, MIDI, WinKeyer or RTDB, whatever the pipeline.

Safety: Nothing Leaves This Device by Default

With This device only, keying during Send Practice does not drive the serial, MIDI, WinKeyer or RTDB outputs, even if they are switched on. Choose Full pipeline only when you mean to send your keying to them. A key wired directly to a transmitter will still transmit. The app cannot stop that, so use a dummy load or disconnect the radio while you practise.

Tips for Straight Keys

  • Paddles key with perfect timing, so they decode reliably from the first character. A straight key is judged by your own timing, which the decoder adapts to over the first few characters, so the first characters of a session may be misread.
  • A pause of about two and a half dots ends a character. If your letters split in two, shorten the pauses between your dots and dashes.
  • If the card says No key input is active, pick at least one key that is switched on in Settings → Other → Send Practice.
Example — Learn Your First Two Characters

Enable Send Practice in Settings → Other. Set Content to Koch method with Lesson 1 (K and M), Count to 1 and Group size to 1. Choose Send practice in the Mode dropdown, press Start, and key the character you see with the space bar (straight key) or the paddle keys. Score above 90 % and the result offers lesson 2.

3.7  E-book Practice

Practise on real, flowing English. Load a book, an article or your own notes and read it in the output area while you listen to it (Copy practice) or key it yourself (Send practice). Choose E-book as the Content of Copy Practice or Send Practice in Settings → Other, load the book there, and pick the practice in the Mode dropdown. The book fills the whole output area on the main screen and in the full-screen view (the Key/Type toggle stays as the practice sets it, and the text blur setting does not apply to a book).

What to Load

FormatGood for
Text (.txt) A novel or story saved as plain text; notes or an article pasted into a text editor; your own practice script; a net or QSO script; a club newsletter.
Markdown (.md) Your own lessons and notes written with headings and lists; README or documentation files; text exported from a notes app.
Web page (.html, .htm) A news or blog article saved with “Save page as”; an offline copy of a web page; a book chapter saved from the web.
EPUB (.epub) A whole book with chapters. Project Gutenberg offers tens of thousands of free books whose copyright has expired, many of them as EPUB: open a book’s page, choose EPUB (no images) or EPUB (with images) under “Download this ebook”, save the file and load it here. The version without images is smaller and plays the same. English classics such as Pride and Prejudice, Alice in Wonderland and the Sherlock Holmes stories are a good start.
Web address A text, Markdown, web page or EPUB file on a server that lets a browser fetch it, for example a raw file on GitHub. Most large sites, including Project Gutenberg, do not allow this: for those, download the file and load it from your device.

Only English text can be played: letters, digits and the punctuation Morse code has. Anything else (symbols, emoji, pictures, other alphabets) is skipped silently and the reading goes on with the next character that can be sent; accented letters are sent as the plain letter. Use only books and texts you are free to read, such as public-domain works and your own files.

The Reading View

The text keeps its letter case, paragraphs, headings and lists. Characters already played or keyed turn another colour and become a little larger, in place, and the character being sent (or waiting for you to key it) is highlighted. No dots and dashes are shown. The text scrolls smoothly so that the current place stays near the middle. When nothing is playing, click or tap a word to start from there. (In Send practice, if the mouse keyer is one of your keys, a click would key, so only the Book button below moves the reading.)

Copy Practice with a Book

Press Start and the book is sent as Morse from where you left off, with the encoder speed and Farnsworth settings. Like someone reading aloud, it pauses a little after a comma or colon, a semicolon and a full stop (also ? and !), and longer at the end of a list item, a paragraph, a heading and a chapter. The pauses are counted in word gaps, so they follow the speed you chose. Playback is timed on the audio clock, so dots, dashes and gaps stay even whatever else the page is doing, and Pause and Resume continue from the character that was interrupted.

Send Practice with a Book

The next character to key is highlighted. Key it and the highlight moves on; a wrong character leaves it where it is until you key the right one. Spaces and line breaks are never keyed. Reading starts with the first key you press (that character counts if it is the right one), and any of the keys you picked may be used, so there is no Start button. Your place is kept, so you can stop whenever you like and key on later; after the end of the book, the next key reads it again from the start. There are no rounds or scores. The same safety rule applies as for any Send practice: with This device only nothing is sent to the serial, MIDI, WinKeyer or RTDB outputs.

The Book Button

The round contents button on the reading view opens the Book dialog. It shows the page you are on (a page is about 1,500 characters, so the numbers are the same on every screen; for text, Markdown and EPUB files the total is an estimate and is shown as “about”), a slider and a box to jump to another page, and Start over. A jump lands at the start of a paragraph and is quick even in a very long book. For an EPUB with a table of contents the dialog also lists the Chapters. Opening the dialog pauses a Copy practice that is playing.

When the book came from a web address, a round reload button (two arrows in a circle) sits just above the contents button, on the main screen and in full screen. It fetches the address again, in case the page has changed, and carries on at the place where you were without asking. If the fetch fails, the book you have stays as it is. It also pauses a Copy practice that is playing.

Resuming

The place where you stopped is remembered for each file or web address, in this browser only, and updated every couple of seconds while you read. When you load the same book again you are asked whether to Resume or start over; choosing Resume closes Settings and takes you back to the practice you loaded the book from. When you start the app, or switch to E-book practice, with no book loaded, the last book opens again by itself at the place where you stopped, without asking. The red × button beside the loaded book in Settings makes the app forget it.

Privacy

A book you choose from your device stays on your device and is read piece by piece as you go. A web address you type is fetched by your browser directly from that site; nothing goes through our servers. So that the last book can open again by itself, the app keeps on this device only that one book (the address, or a copy of the file, up to 100 MB); the red × button deletes it. The book is never sent anywhere and is not included in presets, exports or saved settings.

Example — Listen to a Classic

Download Alice in Wonderland as an EPUB (no images) from Project Gutenberg. In Settings → Other → Copy Practice, set Content to E-book, press Choose file and pick the EPUB, then choose Copy practice in the Mode dropdown and press Start. Watch the text light up as you copy it by ear; use the Book button to continue another day where you stopped.
